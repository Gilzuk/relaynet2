\chapter{Discussion and Conclusions}\label{sec:discussion-and-conclusions}

The canonical, coded and unknown-channel studies have distinct models, endpoints and uncertainty units; they do not form one causal experiment. Chapter~\ref{sec:summary} records the hypothesis outcomes.

\section{Interpretation of Results}\label{sec:interpretation-of-results}

\subsection{Low-SNR Advantage over AF (H1)}\label{sec:low-snr-advantage-of-neural-relays-h1-confirmed}

The historical feedforward relays outperform AF at 0--4~dB (H1; Table~\ref{tbl:table2}). The implemented AF normalizes the zero-forced observation by whole-block power (Section~\ref{sec:evaluated-configurations}); its BER depends on block length: at 0~dB it is $0.387$ for 50-symbol blocks, $0.407$ at the canonical 5{,}000 and $0.413$ at 50{,}000~\coderef{af-block}. A conventional finite-power AF relay, which forwards the unequalized signal with variable gain $\sqrt{P/(|h_1|^{2}+N_0)}$ and is detected coherently at the destination, reaches $0.342$ at 0~dB, $0.245$ at 4~dB and $0.144$ at 8~dB, agreeing with its closed form to within $0.001$ at every SNR. A fixed-gain AF (average rather than instantaneous $|h_1|^2$) does better at 0 and 2~dB ($0.338$ and $0.294$) and worse from 4~dB. Against the variable-gain reference the MLP's margin at 0~dB falls from $0.073$ to $0.007$, still outside the MLP's 95\% interval, and is about $0.02$ at 4--8~dB; against the fixed-gain one it is $0.003$ at 0~dB, just outside that interval, which omits training variance, and inside the VAE's. The sequence relays are worse than either conventional AF at 0 and 4~dB ($0.401$--$0.407$ and $0.276$--$0.281$). The feedforward relays track DF without a resolved advantage. Regeneration is a plausible explanation, but no learning-specific mechanism is isolated; the implemented AF overstated the H1 margin.

A soft mapping is a plausible interpretation, not an isolated mechanism. For unit-energy BPSK in real AWGN the posterior mean is $\tanh(y/\sigma^2)$, or $\tanh(2y)$ at $E_b/N_0=0$~dB. This minimizes local MSE, not necessarily destination BER after normalization and Hop~2. Canonical QPSK/Rayleigh estimation also depends on fading reliability: each equalized real-axis variance is $N_0/(2|h|^2)$. Without $|h|$, the Bayes target averages over that uncertainty. The relays instead train on BPSK/AWGN (Section~\ref{sec:training-protocol}); H1--H4 test transfer to fading. The CSI-assisted AF expression $\gamma_1\gamma_2/(\gamma_1+\gamma_2+1)$ does not describe the implemented block-normalized AF.

\subsection{Medium-to-High-SNR Ordering (H2)}\label{sec:classical-dominance-at-high-snr-h2-confirmed}

In the historical comparison, DF matches or exceeds the learned relays from 6~dB upward. Componentwise slicing is locally MAP, not proven optimal for destination BER over all power-constrained relay mappings. Tanh saturation does not isolate the cause of finite-SNR penalties.

\subsection{Robustness Across the Sampled Ensemble}\label{sec:channel-robustness}

The BPSK/AWGN training settings are in Table~\ref{tbl:canonical-runs}. Rayleigh testing establishes performance over the sampled draws, not every fade or an untested fading law. The equalizer uses CSI; the learned relay receives no explicit coefficient and does not adapt online.

\subsection{Additional Training Replications and Seed Sensitivity}\label{sec:round2-rerun-validation}

Additional training replications use the original budgets: native/3K canonical training, all 36 sequence-memory initializations and matched coding~\coderef{round2-rerun}. Classical control curves reproduced exactly. Differences between trained models do not by themselves invalidate the original measurements, which remain unchanged. These comparisons vary training realizations and execution environment together, so they do not isolate a seed-only effect. The multi-seed sequence study repeats IDs 0, 1 and 2 in another environment; these are not six independent seeds. The reproduction procedure is documented in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-c-software-architecture}.

In the native rerun, the VAE--DF BER gap is $0.00640$ at $4$~dB, whereas the historical realization stayed within $0.004$. Some canonical pointwise rankings change. Fresh paired tests resolve no learned advantage over DF, but condition on each trained checkpoint and do not establish equivalence. On QPSK ISI, the Transformer's best common-target penalty changes from $5.959$ to $6.636$~dB. Mamba-2's initialization spread changes from $>3.282$ to $0.973$~dB: the previously unsuccessful seed now reaches $10^{-3}$. The sequence-memory best-of-three ordering is unchanged on all three channels; these observations do not establish population rankings or stable initialization variances.

For the coded rerun, matched block DF again beats both MLP read-outs in all $30$ paired trials at each of the $6$ SNRs, under either destination metric. Recomputed two-sided Wilcoxon tests give $p<10^{-5}$, uncorrected for multiplicity. The sequence and NumPy-coded paths retain their declared seeds (coded initialization: 0); attributing their differences solely to changed initial seeds is unsupported. Separately, two full-budget native MLP runs with all RNGs seeded to $42$ and deterministic operations produced identical weights and all $110$ trial BERs on one host. This same-seed control establishes repeatability for that configuration and host, not historical recovery. The minimum-size training grid, pilot studies, adaptive tails and matched Mamba were not rerun.

\section{Capacity, Context and Training Variation}\label{sec:capacity-and-overfitting}
\label{sec:information-theoretic-perspective}

The fixed-checkpoint comparison cannot separate architecture from initialization and training. Neither it nor the size sweep measures the train/test decomposition needed to establish overfitting.

\subsection{Reading the Replicated Size Sweep}\label{sec:complexity-curve-turn}

The three-initialization sweep does not demonstrate the capacity-induced BER upturn proposed by H3. Its median across-initialization spread is $0.176$~dB over 148 configurations, but varies from $0.008$~dB on the canonical channel to $1.231$~dB on nonlinear bias. The pooled median is not a universal significance threshold. Two clusters of training outcomes do not by themselves identify optimization attractors or prove a mechanism.

Four-parameter configurations satisfy the zero-degradation criterion on three memoryless cases; selected three-tap cases use 73--145 parameters. These are empirical grid results, not architecture lower bounds. The coded size sweep spans 18--1{,}076 parameters with penalties under its hard read-out of at least $+9.75$~dB against matched block DF, and of $+1.24$--$+1.92$~dB against the unweighted block DF of the diagnostic pipeline. This motivates testing interfaces and metrics, but does not prove an interface-limited regime.

\subsection{Practical Implications}\label{sec:practical-implications}

The canonical model stores 169 float32 weights (676 bytes); the BPSK fixed-ISI network has 170 parameters, and the coded-QPSK network has 220 parameters and 208 MACs per symbol. Storage omits activations, buffers and software; hardware efficiency remains unmeasured.

\subsection{Latency and Adaptivity}\label{sec:latency-and-adaptivity-considerations}

Readiness, computation and protocol scheduling are separate costs. Future-sample and frame buffering may overlap listening, but half-duplex forwarding uses both hops; no end-to-end latency saving was measured.

In Section~\ref{sec:joint-latency-memory}, no BER difference is resolved among the tested traceback settings. A two-symbol MLSE setting has less look-ahead than the five-symbol windowed relay and lower measured BER. The exactly factorized trellis needs fewer multiplications than the network up to six taps and 1.23 times as many at seven, and MLSE remains more accurate (the implemented joint trellis crosses it between three and five taps). Neither comparison establishes a hardware latency advantage.

Neither the learned relay nor the canonical experiments demonstrate online adaptation. AF's gain is recalculated from block power, and DF requires no training; neither is a fixed-SNR baseline.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Experimental scope.} One two-hop SISO relay and no direct path are modeled. The canonical comparison uses uncoded QPSK; BPSK, DBPSK, faded-ISI QPSK and coded modulation studies are separate. The QPSK ISI re-check changes both modulation and fading. The 16-QAM coded grid is not a learned-relay architecture study.
\item \textbf{Model information.} Canonical receivers have perfect CSI. Pilot-limited and blind studies relax this within specified families. Training supplies family knowledge even when no analytic parameters or pilots are given at inference.
\item \textbf{Finite data and replication.} Fixed checkpoints, three-seed studies, paired trial tests and pooled-bit bounds have different uncertainty units. Nominal independent-bit intervals need not have their stated coverage under correlated decoding errors. Zero errors mean insufficient data for a positive BER estimate, not zero true BER. MLP fixed-budget ISI validation stops at 16~dB.
\item \textbf{Comparator scope.} Matched Viterbi optimizes sequence error. The QPSK bit-MAP control measures only relay-output BER, not destination BER. Reduced-state MLSE and decision feedback are unmeasured alternatives, as is a coded destination metric that also models relay decision errors; for relays that make decisions, the matched destination metric of Section~\ref{sec:coded-matched-metrics} weights by the second hop only.
\item \textbf{Coded metrics.} Matched decoding reverses the original MLP advantage over block DF (Section~\ref{sec:coded-matched-metrics}); coded Mamba was not rerun. Secondary coded studies also include matched comparisons. The coded size sweep reruns only block DF and retains the learned relays' unweighted destination decoder; adaptation envelopes are derived from the corresponding saved curves. Matched block DF is stronger throughout; only the structural lock-out at a 100-symbol readiness budget survives unchanged.
\item \textbf{Generalization and hardware.} Memory is swept only through seven taps. New impairment families, correlated Doppler fading, other topologies, complete deadline models, quantized execution, energy and actual device timing are outside the evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future-work}

Priority controls are end-to-end bit-MAP with consistent information and normalization, relay-error-aware destination metrics, and prospective training-seed replication. BPSK bit-MAP and plain-QPSK ISI under Section~\ref{sec:qpsk-unknown-channel}'s protocol would address the endpoint and modulation confounds. Larger constellations need multilevel outputs; broader generalization and hardware evaluation remain open.

\section{Hypotheses Revisited}\label{sec:conclusions}

H1 and H2 are supported within the canonical comparison. H3's overfitting explanation is not established. H4 does not hold across SNR: the low-SNR BER gap between the model families persists, and the high-SNR convergence is not robust across all measured seeds. In the equal-budget replication, Mamba spreads are $0.019$ and $0.041$~dB, whereas the Transformer's is at least $0.89$~dB. These are three-seed observations, not universal architecture variances (Table~\ref{tbl:seed-spread-3k}).

Among eight Transformer initializations, one has higher final loss and worse high-SNR BER. Three penalties are lower bounds, so a correlation treating them as exact is unsupported. Neither a population failure rate nor a screening rule is established (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-f-minimum-size}).

H5 is supported in the evaluated settings against AF and zero-threshold DF, which the chosen taps defeat analytically; the informative evidence is against model-based detectors: matched BPSK MLSE keeps penalties of $0.26$--$2.53$~dB, depending on the target, and the MLP leads LS-plus-MLSE at five pilots and leads blind CMA up to 12~dB. Similar rounded BERs and overlapping intervals elsewhere do not establish equivalence. Small trained relays help in the evaluated mismatched pipelines; their measured benefits depend on the available information, context, metrics and budgets.
