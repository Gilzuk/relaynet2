\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:appendix-c-software-architecture}

\subsection{Testing} Automated pytest tests cover channels, modulation, coding, relay strategies, weight persistence, simulation, statistics and verification. Record the environment and skipped tests when repeating the suite; four tests require the optional Mamba-2 module. The canonical runs did not retain their historical library versions.

\subsection{Reproducibility} Evaluation seeds are controlled for source-bit and noise generation. The canonical training never seeds PyTorch's weight initialization (Section~\ref{sec:training-protocol}), so the original checkpoints cannot be retrained exactly; the later replicated sweeps report training-seed variation explicitly.

\paragraph{Provenance of the reported results.} Each reported result is associated with an experiment artifact in the source-code repository, using the experiment-specific budgets documented in the relevant section and ledger. Registered experiments are linked to their generating scripts and committed result files by a provenance audit~\coderef{prov-audit}, and a table verifier~\coderef{verifier} checks a declared subset of numerical table cells against those files; neither covers every number, figure or prose statement (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-e-master-experiment-ledger}). Independent audit requires that repository at the archived revision identifier; the ledger below documents the claimed provenance but cannot by itself reproduce every table cell.

\section{Minimum Relay Size and Initialization Variance}\label{sec:appendix-f-minimum-size}

Supporting material for the minimum-size study of Section~\ref{sec:minimum-relay-size} and the initialization-variance measurements discussed in Section~\ref{sec:capacity-and-overfitting}. The findings themselves are stated in those sections; what follows is the underlying tabulation and the figures, collected here to keep the experiment and discussion chapters to their conclusions.

\paragraph{Sweep configuration.} Every channel is run on a width and window grid and by width bisection at depths 1--3~\coderef{minsize}, with the models of Table~\ref{tbl:minsize-channels}. Each configuration is trained three times, from initialization seeds 0, 1 and 2, on $60{,}000$ symbols drawn from its own Hop-1 channel, $20{,}000$ at each of 5, 10 and 15~dB (both axes of each QPSK symbol are used), for 25 epochs with batch size 256 and learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$; the network after the final epoch is evaluated. Evaluation uses 20 trials of $20{,}000$ bits at 0--20~dB in 4-dB steps, with the same bits and channel draws for every relay. Windows are completed with zeros at the trial edges; QPSK channels are processed per axis. Width runs from 1 to 48 hidden units and the window from 1 to 7 samples. Penalties follow Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-g-channel-specs}: a candidate's worst-of-seeds BER at each SNR against its reference relay's mean-of-seeds curve.

Eight Transformer initializations are shown in the following figures: seven cluster and one has both higher final loss and worse high-SNR BER. A sample of three cannot reliably characterize such failures. If three runs were sampled without replacement from these eight, the probability of missing the one outlier would be $\binom{7}{3}/\binom{8}{3}=5/8$; this finite-set calculation is not an estimate of the population failure rate. The association does not validate training loss as a general screening rule.

\section{Verification of Headline Claims}\label{sec:appendix-claim-verification}

Each headline claim of Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} is restated here with the argument or derivation that supports it and the primary reference against which it was checked. The fixed-ISI channel is the normalized 3-tap FIR $\mathbf{h} = [1, 0.7, 0.5]/\lVert\cdot\rVert \approx [0.758, 0.531, 0.379]$.

\subsection{Matched-receiver bounds and the empirical MLSE comparison}

For a fixed observation and supplied model, bit-MAP minimizes expected bit error; MLSE instead minimizes sequence error \cite{Forney1972MLSE,BahlCockeJelinekRaviv1974BCJR,ProakisSalehi2008DigitalComm}. Thus a lower measured BER than MLSE would not contradict its optimality. The QPSK BCJR control measures relay-output errors only and must not be compared directly with destination BER. It does not close an end-to-end BCJR benchmark. Learned-receiver studies use different information assumptions and objectives \cite{FarsadGoldsmith2018NNDetection,Shlezinger2020ViterbiNet,Honkala2021DeepRx,YeLiJuang2018OFDMDL,OSheaHoydis2017PhysicalLayer}; they should not be summarized as a universal claim that learning wins only under mismatch.

\subsection{The analytic 0.25 floor and the non-monotonicity of symbol-wise DF}

With the normalized taps, the interferer magnitude sum $h_1 + h_2 \approx 0.910$ exceeds the cursor $h_0 \approx 0.758$: the eye is closed. Of the four equiprobable BPSK interferer sign patterns, exactly one (both opposing the cursor) yields a noise-free sample of $0.758 - 0.910 = -0.152 < 0$, a deterministic sign flip, while the remaining three ($0.606$, $0.910$, $1.668$) preserve the sign -- so a memoryless slicer errs on exactly one pattern in four as noise vanishes, giving the analytic floor of exactly $1/4$. The non-monotonicity follows from the same geometry: at moderate SNR, noise occasionally pushes the flipped sample back across the decision boundary, so the conditional error rate on that pattern rises toward one as SNR grows and total BER climbs toward $0.25$ from below -- standard behavior for a closed-eye channel under symbol-by-symbol detection \cite{LuckySalzWeldon1968, ProakisSalehi2008DigitalComm}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Complexity: the $M^{L}$ trellis against a fixed forward pass}{Complexity: the M to the L trellis against a fixed forward pass}}
An unrestricted joint-symbol MLSE implementation maintains $M^{L-1}$ states and evaluates $M^L$ branch metrics for channel length $L$ \cite{Forney1972MLSE,ProakisSalehi2008DigitalComm}. For the real-tap QPSK channel, exact I/Q factorization reduces that search to two binary trellises, and for square $M$-QAM to two $\sqrt{M}$-ary trellises with $2M^{L/2}$ branch metrics (Section~\ref{sec:iq-mlse-factorization}); the joint count is not a necessary cost of matched detection. The separate real $11\to13\to1$ MLP has $170$ parameters and $11\cdot13+13=156$ MACs, plus 14 bias additions and nonlinear activations. Counting a multiply and add as two FLOPs gives approximately 330 FLOPs excluding activation cost. Cost is fixed only for that deployed architecture; longer windows increase it. Reduced-state sequence estimation \cite{EyuboqluQureshi1988RSSE} is another unmeasured lower-cost classical alternative.

\subsection{The blind regime: CMA against decision-directed MLSE}

CMA is a blind equalizer for constant-modulus signals \cite{Godard1980SelfRecovering,TreichlerAgee1983CMA}. The measured random composite family also contains nonlinearity and phase uncertainty, so the zeros of one fixed linear tap vector do not establish invertibility or convergence for the whole cascade. The observed CMA and decision-directed MLSE results are implementation-specific. Documented convergence and decision-feedback issues \cite{Ding1991IllConvergence,RaheliPolydorosTzou1995PSP} motivate possible explanations but do not identify the cause of these measurements.

\subsection{The pilot-budget crossover: resolved between twenty and five pilots, with severe degradation at five}

A three-tap least-squares fit can be identifiable from five pilots if its design matrix has full column rank. For a correctly specified linear model with white noise, its covariance is $\sigma^2(\mathbf X^H\mathbf X)^{-1}$ \cite{Kay1993Estimation}; the simplification $\sigma^2 L/N_p$ additionally requires suitably normalized orthogonal pilot columns. The nonlinear composite channel need not satisfy that model. The observed small-pilot degradation is therefore not a measured rank threshold or an unbounded-error theorem. Other estimators, including meta-learned demodulation \cite{ParkSimeone2021MetaDemod}, are not benchmarked here.

\subsection{The finite-window gap is not causally decomposed}

For bit-MAP decisions under the same model and side information, giving the detector a full sequence cannot increase its minimum expected bit error relative to a window. This observation-ordering argument is not a bound on a particular Viterbi-versus-MLP BER gap, because Viterbi optimizes sequence error. No windowed bit-MAP ablation separates truncation, approximation, finite training, and optimization effects here. The BPSK required-SNR penalties range from $0.26$ to $2.53$~dB at the stated targets. They cannot be attributed solely to the 170-parameter budget.

\paragraph{Supplementary numerical checks.} The across-seed spread in SNR penalty against DF in the separate native-architecture replication is $0.004$~dB for Mamba-S6, $0.007$ for the Hybrid, $0.013$ for Mamba-2, $0.043$ for the MLP, and at least $0.075$ for the Transformer and $0.091$ for the VAE (one initialization of each misses $10^{-2}$). These finite three-seed ranges are not confidence bounds on failure probabilities.

For the QPSK linear-MMSE detail run, the attained MSE at 16~dB is $0.0666,\allowbreak\ 0.0374,\allowbreak\ 0.0354,\allowbreak\ 0.0333$ for $N=3,5,7,11$. The required-SNR penalties at targets $10^{-1}$ and $10^{-2}$ are respectively $+3.27, +3.01, +2.82, +2.68$ and $+6.35, +4.28, +4.14, +4.03$~dB against the size sweep's MLSE curve. These measurements do not imply monotonic BER at every target.

\paragraph{Amplifier.} $A$ acts on the amplitude and preserves the phase: $A(r) = r/\sqrt{1 + (r/1.2)^{2}}$, a Rapp soft limiter with smoothness $1$ and saturation $1.2$. Because the ISI taps are real and the DBPSK symbols are $\pm1$, the amplifier input is real, so the rotation $e^{j\theta}$ applied after it is the only source of an imaginary component on Hop~1.

\paragraph{Normalization and noise.} Relay outputs are scaled to unit power over the evaluation trial (Eq.~\eqref{eq:power-normalization}). In the flat study the Hop-2 noise of real forwarded signals originally had variance $1/\gamma$, twice the convention above, while AF in the flat-phase case forwards a complex signal and received $1/(2\gamma)$ per dimension. The study was rerun with $1/(2\gamma)$ for every relay on the original seeds; Table~\ref{tbl:tableE6flat} reports the rerun. DF and the MLP had always seen the same Hop-2 noise, so their comparison was unaffected; their absolute BERs roughly halved.

\paragraph{Pilots.} Pilot symbols are the first $N_p$ transmitted symbols of a trial or block, which are known to the estimator. The estimator fits three complex taps by least squares to a linear model, so it does not model the amplifier. In the fixed-composite study ($N_p = 200$) the decoded pilot region is scored together with the payload; in the pilot-budget sweep it is excluded, and in its block-length panel every block of $L$ symbols carries $10$ pilots and its own channel draw.

\paragraph{Windows and their boundaries.} The learned relays of the uncoded Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} studies read a window of $W = 11$ samples centered on the decided symbol (the coded-memory study sweeps $W$ from $1$ to $21$, the size sweep from $1$ to $7$, and the two Mamba relays of Section~\ref{sec:sequence-on-memory} use only the causal half of theirs); on complex channels the $I$ and $Q$ windows are concatenated into $2W$ inputs. The NumPy MLPs complete trial-end windows with zeros. The sequence-on-memory study zero-pads training windows but repeats edge samples in the inherited inference wrappers (Section~\ref{sec:sequence-on-memory}); canonical inference also repeats the edge sample.

\paragraph{DBPSK.} The source bits $x_i \in \{\pm1\}$ are differentially encoded as $s_i = s_{i-1}x_i$. The learned relay is trained with the information bit $x_i$ as its target, so it estimates $x_i$ directly rather than the differential symbol; its real output, scaled to unit power, is forwarded as a BPSK signal, and the destination decides on the sign of its real part without differential decoding. The DF relay performs differential detection, $\hat{x}_i = \operatorname{sign}\operatorname{Re}\{y_i y_{i-1}^{*}\}$, and forwards $\hat{x}_i$; AF forwards the complex observation and the destination performs the differential detection. The first bit, the differential reference, is excluded from scoring, except for the learned relay in the fixed-composite study, where it is scored (one bit per $10^{5}$).

\paragraph{Training.} Each learned relay is trained once, at SNR $5$, $10$ and $15$~dB in equal proportions, on data drawn from the Hop-1 model of its own study, with fixed sample counts and epochs (for example $1.2\times10^{5}$ windows and $25$ epochs for the fixed ISI, and $1.6\times10^{5}$ windows and $30$ epochs for the composite studies). The network used for evaluation is the one left after the final epoch; no validation-based checkpoint selection or early stopping is applied. The sequence-model wrappers of the canonical comparison differ: they restore the epoch of lowest training loss, under a $10^{-5}$ improvement threshold (Section~\ref{sec:training-protocol}).

\paragraph{Single-number SNR penalties.} All required-SNR penalties use one definition~\coderef{ber-metrics}. For a BER target $t$, the SNR at which a curve reaches $t$ is found by linear interpolation of $\ln \mathrm{BER}$ against SNR in dB between the two measured points that bracket $t$, taking the last such crossing. A zero-error point cannot be interpolated in log scale: if the preceding point lies above $t$, the crossing is placed at the zero-error point's SNR, which can only overstate the SNR the curve needs. The penalty at $t$ is the relay's SNR minus the reference's, positive when the relay needs more. The targets are fixed by the reference curve: of $10^{-1}$, $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$, those the reference reaches within the tested SNR range form a common set for every curve compared against it. A curve that misses a target in that set needs more than the highest tested SNR there, so its penalty at that target is only bounded below, by that SNR minus the reference's, and is marked as a lower bound rather than dropped. Tables~\ref{tbl:mmse-baseline}, \ref{tbl:seq-on-memory}, \ref{tbl:table-minsize}, \ref{tbl:seed-spread} and~\ref{tbl:seed-spread-3k} report one number per configuration: the largest penalty over the common set, itself a lower bound when a target is missed. Their aggregation over training seeds differs, as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item Table~\ref{tbl:mmse-baseline}: every column against the size sweep's MLSE curve; the learned-relay column is the best of three seeds of the best size-sweep configuration.
\item Table~\ref{tbl:seq-on-memory}: the best of three seeds, with the spread in brackets.
\item Table~\ref{tbl:table-minsize}: the candidate's worst-of-seeds BER at each SNR, against the reference relay's mean-of-seeds curve; a candidate with only a lower bound meets no budget.
\item Tables~\ref{tbl:seed-spread} and~\ref{tbl:seed-spread-3k}: the largest minus the smallest per-seed penalty, a lower bound when a seed is bounded.
\end{itemize}
They are recomputed from the stored per-seed curves~\coderef{common-targets}; the same script reproduces the earlier values, which kept only the targets both curves reach. The fixed-ISI comparison with genie MLSE is reported per target ($10^{-1}$ to $10^{-4}$), computed from the matched-protocol data~\coderef{isi-penalty}.
